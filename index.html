The project / 01

Your people. Your resources.
A connection on your terms.

Your team already has a work identity. SupiVPN connects that identity to VPN access: browser-based SAML sign-in, groups and session rules, with OpenVPN Community carrying the traffic.

Built in Go and self-hosted on your infrastructure, SupiVPN gives you control over access decisions. Group IP pools let your network rules distinguish traffic by team.

The architecture / 04

Different tools.
One access model.

Your identity provider knows your people. SupiVPN decides who gets access. The VPN carries the traffic. Clear responsibilities give us room to add more ways to connect the pieces.

Explore the diagram: hover, focus or tap a component. Press Escape or tap outside to close its details.

Today

Bring your SAML IdP.

Okta is a setup example, not a requirement. Standard SAML IdPs can supply the verified identity and groups that SupiVPN uses.

Planned

More ways to sign in.

Additional authentication protocols around the same access-policy model. SAML is the current implementation.

Planned

More storage choices.

Additional state backends with the same consistency guarantees. PostgreSQL is the shared store available today.

New protocols and storage backends still need implementation and testing. They are not configuration options yet.

A few good questions / 07

Before you dive in.

Who is SupiVPN for?

Teams exploring self-hosted access to private networks and services using their existing work identity. SupiVPN brings SAML sign-in, group policies and session controls to OpenVPN Community. You operate the infrastructure and configure its routing and firewall rules.

How does sign-in work?

The VPN client opens a browser sign-in with your identity provider. SupiVPN verifies the SAML response and evaluates group and session policy. Its Go agent applies the decision on the VPN node; OpenVPN carries the tunnel traffic. See the technical architecture.

Which identity providers and protocols are supported?

SAML is the authentication protocol implemented today, with Okta as the documented setup example. Other SAML providers need interoperability checks against the IdP requirements. Additional authentication protocols and storage backends are planned; PostgreSQL is the current shared store.

Can I use it in production today?

The Debian deployment guide covers a minimal setup for evaluation. Production deployment guidance, active-active HA deployment instructions and infrastructure as code are planned. Review the current limitations and security model before choosing it for a deployment.

Can the database live on another host?

Yes. The controller can use an external PostgreSQL server with verified TLS and separate runtime and migration identities. On v0.6.0, use the manual setup; remote database support in the setup helper is on main and pending release. Follow the external database guide for the requirements and migration steps.

What happens if a VPN node fails?

Existing tunnels do not move between nodes. A client connecting to another node must sign in again and receives a different VPN IP; existing TCP and SSH sessions are lost. Active-active nodes provide another place to connect, not seamless session failover. See the failure behavior.

Is SupiVPN open source? How can I help?

Yes. SupiVPN is licensed under AGPL-3.0-only. You can read the code, contribute improvements, share your use case or support development. Sponsorship is voluntary and includes no services, priority support or exclusive features.
